Flagship Governance Frameworks

AGCIH’s core institutional frameworks for governing high-impact digital and AI-enabled public systems.

AGCIH’s frameworks support governments and public institutions to move from digital and AI ambition to responsible, trusted implementation. They focus on institutional readiness, leadership oversight, and operational governance — ensuring that high-impact systems strengthen accountability, legitimacy, and public value.

These frameworks do not replace regulators or technology teams. They strengthen governance architecture: mandates, decision rights, oversight pathways, risk escalation, and accountability mechanisms.

Foundational Thinking

AGCIH frameworks are grounded in rule-of-law accountability for automated public authority.

Knowledge Paper 001 — Rule of Law in the Age of Agentic AI

This paper explains why agentic AI changes the governance question in government: it can plan, act, and execute, creating diffusion of agency and blurred responsibility if accountability infrastructure is not rebuilt.

It introduces five rule-of-law tests that inform AGCIH’s frameworks: legality, accountability, reasons-giving, contestability, and auditability.

The Three Flagship Frameworks

Together, these frameworks form a coherent governance system: readiness → accountability → operational control.

1) Institutional AI Governance Readiness Framework (I-AIGR)

AGCIH’s anchor framework — assessing whether an institution is governance-ready to deploy, oversee, or rely on high-impact AI or automated decision systems.

Core Governance Question:
Is the institution itself ready to govern AI responsibly — beyond strategy and technology readiness?

What it clarifies:

  • Mandate and authority for AI-enabled decisions
  • Accountability and oversight pathways
  • Risk identification and escalation mechanisms
  • Institutional capacity for governance decisions
  • Public trust, legitimacy, and redress readiness

Best used when: an institution is preparing to adopt, scale, or supervise AI-enabled systems and needs a defensible readiness baseline.

2) Board & Executive Oversight Framework (BEO-HIDS)

The leadership accountability framework — supporting boards and senior executives to govern AI-enabled and high-impact digital systems with clarity, responsibility, and confidence.

Core Governance Question:
Do leadership structures have clear oversight, decision rights, and accountability when digital systems affect rights, services, or public trust?

What it strengthens:

  • Leadership decision rights and escalation routes
  • Risk ownership and non-delegable governance responsibilities
  • Assurance pathways for harm, bias, and failures
  • Accountability and consequence when boundaries are crossed

Best used when: boards, accounting officers, or executives require a clear governance posture and defensible oversight responsibilities.

3) Operational AI Governance & Control Framework (O-AIGC)

The operationalisation framework — translating AI strategies, ethics commitments, and policies into day-to-day institutional controls and governance practice.

Core Governance Question:
What changes inside an institution so governance commitments become operational controls and institutional practice?

What it enables:

  • Clear roles, responsibilities, and authority boundaries
  • Governance checkpoints before, during, and after deployment
  • Risk monitoring and response pathways
  • Documentation and traceability for accountability

Best used when: an institution needs to translate policies into operational governance without creating parallel bureaucracy or symbolic checklists.

How the Frameworks Work Together

A single governance system designed for repeated institutional deployment.

Readiness

I-AIGR establishes whether an institution is governance-ready.

Accountability

BEO-HIDS clarifies leadership oversight and decision rights.

Operational Control

O-AIGC operationalises governance into institutional practice.

AGCIH publishes framework logic and high-level governance principles publicly, while reserving detailed diagnostics, maturity logic, templates, and implementation instruments for structured institutional engagements.
