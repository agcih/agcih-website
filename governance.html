Governance Framework

AGCIH Governance & Board Charter

The Africa Governance & Civic Innovation Hub (AGCIH) is established as a governance and public-sector innovation trust. This Governance & Board Charter summarises how the organisation is led, how the Board of Trustees functions and how we safeguard integrity, accountability and the public interest.

1. Purpose

Purpose of the Board

The Board of Trustees provides independent governance and strategic oversight to the Africa Governance & Civic Innovation Hub (AGCIH). The Board safeguards AGCIH’s mission, ensures alignment with its founding Trust Deed and upholds standards of integrity, accountability and public interest.

In doing so, the Board helps ensure that AGCIH’s work at the intersection of governance, AI, digital transformation and civic innovation remains principled, evidence-informed and responsive to Zimbabwe’s institutional realities.

2. Mandate

Role and Responsibilities

The Board of Trustees:

  • Approves AGCIH’s strategic direction, thematic priorities and major programmes.
  • Oversees financial stewardship, risk management and compliance with the Trust Deed and applicable laws.
  • Ensures that AGCIH’s work reflects its values: governance, human rights, inclusion, ethics and public-sector innovation.
  • Appoints, supports and provides oversight to the Executive Director.
  • Reviews organisational performance, major partnerships and key institutional risks.
  • Ensures that AGCIH’s engagement with government, regulators, partners and communities is non-partisan, professional and constructive.
3. Composition

Board Composition & Expertise

The Board is multi-disciplinary by design. It brings together expertise that reflects the core dimensions of AGCIH’s work and the governance responsibilities of a public-interest trust.

The founding Board includes dedicated seats for:

  • Legal, Governance & Compliance
  • Gender, Inclusion & Equity
  • Digital Transformation & AI Governance
  • Finance, Audit & Risk Oversight
  • Youth, Innovation & Future Readiness

As AGCIH grows, additional expertise in public policy, research and state capability may be added, in line with the organisation’s strategic needs and regulatory requirements.

4. Independence

Independence & Integrity

Board members serve in their personal capacity, not as formal delegates of any institution. They are expected to:

  • Disclose and manage potential conflicts of interest.
  • Uphold confidentiality where required.
  • Act at all times in the best interests of AGCIH’s mission, beneficiaries and the broader public interest.

The Board collectively upholds a culture of integrity, transparency and responsible stewardship, consistent with AGCIH’s focus on governance and accountability.

5. Executive Leadership

Relationship with the Executive Director

The Executive Director leads AGCIH’s strategy, operations, partnerships and external engagement. The Board provides oversight, guidance and support to the Executive Director, without interfering in day-to-day management.

The relationship between the Board and the Executive Director is one of partnership, trust and constructive challenge. The Board holds the Executive Director accountable for agreed results and for upholding AGCIH’s values, while enabling the flexibility needed to innovate and respond to emerging governance and technology issues.

6. Meetings

Board Meetings & Decision-Making

The Board meets a minimum of [X] times per year, with additional meetings convened as needed to address strategic or risk-related matters. Meetings may be held in person or virtually, in accordance with the Trust Deed and applicable regulations.

In general, the Board aims to:

  • Make decisions by consensus wherever possible.
  • Use formal voting, in line with the Trust Deed, where consensus cannot be reached and decisions are time-sensitive.
  • Keep clear records of decisions, resolutions and follow-up actions to support institutional memory and accountability.
7. Inclusion & Responsible Technology

Commitment to Inclusion & Responsible Technology

Given AGCIH’s focus on governance, AI and digital transformation, the Board recognises that technology choices have deep institutional, social and ethical implications. The Board therefore commits to:

  • Promoting gender equality, youth participation and inclusion in AGCIH’s governance and programming.
  • Ensuring that AGCIH’s work on AI, data and digital systems is rights-respecting, ethical and grounded in African realities and values.
  • Anticipating long-term institutional and societal impacts of emerging technologies on public administration, accountability and civic space.
  • Encouraging open, multi-stakeholder dialogue on AI governance, digital rights and public-sector innovation.

Through this Charter, the Board expresses its intention to steward AGCIH as a trusted, principled and future-focused institution serving Zimbabwe and the wider region.

This Governance & Board Charter is a public summary for transparency and partner engagement. More detailed provisions are contained in AGCIH’s Trust Deed and any related internal governance instruments.
