Danai Hazel Kudya is the Founder and Executive Director of the Africa Governance & Civic Innovation Hub (AGCIH), a governance and public-sector innovation trust focused on responsible AI governance, digital transformation and system-level strengthening in Zimbabwe and across Africa. Her career spans more than 15 years across national human rights institutions, anti-corruption organisations, peacebuilding bodies and the United Nations, with a consistent focus on making public institutions more accountable, transparent and people-centred.

Before establishing AGCIH, Danai served as a Human Rights Officer with the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) in Liberia, where she worked on rule of law, access to justice, accountability, civic space and support to national institutions. Prior to that, she held senior roles with Zimbabwe’s National Peace and Reconciliation Commission and the Zimbabwe Human Rights Commission, and led anti-corruption and legal advocacy work at Transparency International Zimbabwe. Across these roles she has worked closely with government ministries, independent commissions, Parliament, civil society and international partners.

Through AGCIH, Danai is deliberately positioning governance, ethics and institutional capacity at the centre of Zimbabwe’s emerging AI and digital transformation agenda. Her work includes developing governance frameworks, readiness tools and practical guidance that help public institutions adopt AI and digital systems responsibly, while protecting rights, guarding against corruption risks and ensuring that technology serves people rather than displacing them.

Danai’s approach to AI governance is grounded in Africa’s lived realities: fragile institutions, constrained resources, complex political economies and deep inequalities. She works to bridge global debates on AI ethics and regulation with Zimbabwe’s own policy processes, including the forthcoming National AI Strategy and broader Smart Zimbabwe 2030 vision, so that national choices are both future-facing and contextually feasible.

As Executive Director, she leads AGCIH’s strategy, partnerships and thought leadership, engaging government, regulators, parliament, independent commissions, civil society, private sector and regional bodies. Her goal is to help build an ecosystem where governance, oversight and Ubuntu-informed values shape how AI and digital systems are designed, procured and deployed across the public sector.

Ms. Shimtah Atipa Nembaware is a distinguished gender, human rights and social inclusion advocate whose work over the past decade has shaped community protection systems, institutional responses and national efforts to address gender-based violence (GBV) in Zimbabwe. In her role as Manager: Complaints Handling and Investigations at the Zimbabwe Gender Commission, she oversees sensitive investigations, coordinates stakeholder engagement and supports the enforcement of women’s rights within a constitutional and governance framework.

Her experience cuts across community programming, private legal practice, advocacy and national human rights institutions (NHRIs). She has led initiatives that empower women and marginalised groups to prevent, identify and respond to GBV, including emerging digital harms such as online abuse, cyber-harassment and technology-facilitated violence. Through this work, she has strengthened pathways for access to justice and contributed to policy dialogues on inclusion, equality and human dignity.

Ms. Nembaware holds a Bachelor of Laws (Honours) Degree from the University of Zimbabwe; a Master of Arts in Women, Children and Nature Rights in Environmental Governance from the University of Nairobi (Kenya); and a Master of Science in Peace, Leadership and Conflict Resolution from the Zimbabwe Open University. She also holds a Post-Graduate Diploma in Higher Education from Chinhoyi University of Technology and an Executive Certificate in Monitoring and Evaluation from the University of Zimbabwe, among several other professional qualifications.

Her contributions to human rights leadership extend beyond Zimbabwe. As a participant in the prestigious International Visitor Leadership Program (IVLP), she gained exposure to global good practices in gender justice, institutional strengthening and governance systems. This global perspective enhances her ability to translate international insights into contextually relevant solutions for Zimbabwe and the broader region.

Within the Africa Governance & Civic Innovation Hub (AGCIH), Ms. Nembaware brings invaluable expertise in gender-responsive governance, safeguarding, inclusion frameworks and the intersection between gender and technology. Her leadership supports AGCIH’s mission to ensure that emerging technologies – including artificial intelligence – are deployed in ways that protect rights, promote equitable participation and strengthen public institutions.
